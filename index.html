Discussion Guide — Marketplace

Session brief for discussion on marketplace concept

Date September 10, 2026
01

What a marketplace and automation could solve

A two-sided marketplace connecting brands with production companies for on-screen product placement. What automation specifically could solve:

  • The expertise and brand relationships are the real asset — staff know which brands fit which shows, productions trust the curation. Automation isn't meant to replace that.
  • Flat subscription revenue ($350–$12K/mo) is also decoupled from placement volume — automation could let growth track demand, not headcount.
02

What's there today, what we've automated

A closer look at how a request happens today, and what the prototype already picks up:

TodayWhat we've built
A brand request starts as a message on the product page, with one structured field (return required).A structured checkout — quantity, project, dates, delivery address, and terms all captured with the request.
Production needs are gathered via a request form into a shared spreadsheet.A production request board — the same intake, live and searchable inside the platform.
Availability is described in the product listing itself.Quantity tracked per product, automatically adjusted as requests are fulfilled.
Terms are agreed through the conversation.A contract/e-signature step built into the request flow.
Brands and productions coordinate shipping directly.Fulfillment/shipping tracking — next up on the roadmap.
03

The opportunity

  • Automate matching, requests, contracts, fulfillment — decouple growth from headcount.
  • Two motions: self-serve long tail + curated placements for premium productions.
  • Transaction data becomes a byproduct of the platform itself.
04

Strategy

  • Automate what a human account manager does today — not a replacement for relationships.
  • Phased: prototype (done) → pilot with trusted partners → real payments/contracts → scale.
  • Land as an efficiency tool before positioning as an open marketplace.
05

Unique value proposition

  • Brands — pay for performance (bid for visibility), not a flat subscription.
  • Productions — self-serve sourcing, no waiting on a human matchmaker.
  • Platform — economics that scale with usage, not staff time.

07

Open strategic questions

  • Companion tool for the existing business, or a standalone offering?
  • Who curates Featured Programs long-term — staff only, or eventually self-serve?
  • Pricing: per-order fee vs. subscription vs. hybrid?
  • Access: exclusive to the existing vetted network, or open more broadly?
  • Liability: who owns a lost/damaged product or an IP/likeness issue?
08

Additional items worth discussing

  • Success metrics — requests fulfilled without staff intervention, brand response rate, time-to-fulfillment vs. today's manual baseline.
  • Pilot structure — 2–3 trusted brand + production partners, time-boxed, specific hypotheses to test.
  • Risks to watch — two-sided cold start; automation undercutting the high-touch feel that's currently a selling point; payment/contract flows raise the compliance bar.
  • Competitive landscape — who else is structuring this space, and what's defensible about existing relationships and curation expertise as a starting point.

11

What's been built — prototype capabilities

  • Accounts & roles: brand, production, admin. Brand product catalog with paid (bid-based) visibility boosting. Discount codes.
  • Structured, extensible checkout: inventory check→discount→platform fee→e-signature→payment session
  • Production Request Board — productions post a need, brands respond, production accepts → real order.
  • Featured Programs — admin curates a show's wishlist, brands respond, admin selects a winner → real order.
  • All flows verified end-to-end.
12

What's next

Real Stripe payments + real e-signature (code ready, need live accounts) → matching/recommendation → brand↔production messaging → fulfillment tracking → polish & infrastructure (hosting, production database, CI).

13

Feedback we want — one use case (e.g. Netflix)

  • Would prop/costume teams actually self-serve a request board, or is a human relationship still required at this scale?
  • What would make them trust a platform-tracked order over today's informal process?
  • Does curated Featured Programs fit a season-long need better than open self-serve?
  • What's realistic economics from their side?
